11. Decision-Making & Problem-Solving

Workplace & Social Skills

Rational Decision-Making Model

  1. Define the Problem — Clearly articulate what needs to be solved
  2. Identify Criteria — What factors matter? (cost, time, quality, impact)
  3. Weight Criteria — Rank criteria by importance
  4. Generate Alternatives — Brainstorm possible solutions
  5. Evaluate Alternatives — Score each against weighted criteria
  6. Select the Best — Choose the highest-scoring option
  7. Implement & Monitor — Execute and track results

Group Decision-Making

Group decisions benefit from diverse perspectives but can suffer from groupthink (desire for harmony overriding critical thinking). Techniques to improve group decisions:

  • Brainstorming — Generate ideas freely, evaluate later
  • Nominal Group Technique — Individuals think silently, then share, then vote
  • Delphi Method — Anonymously gather expert opinions over multiple rounds
  • Devil's Advocate — Assign someone to challenge assumptions

Creative Problem-Solving

The Creative Problem Solving (CPS) process:

  1. Clarify — Identify the goal, gather data, frame the problem
  2. Ideate — Generate many ideas, defer judgment
  3. Develop — Refine and combine ideas into solutions
  4. Implement — Plan and execute the solution

Critical Thinking

Critical thinking involves analyzing facts to form a judgment. Key skills:

  • Questioning — Challenge assumptions and ask "why"
  • Evidence Evaluation — Distinguish fact from opinion
  • Identifying Bias — Recognize your own and others' biases
  • Logical Reasoning — Draw conclusions from evidence
  • Considering Alternatives — Explore multiple perspectives

Common Decision-Making Pitfalls

  • Anchoring Bias — Over-relying on the first piece of information
  • Confirmation Bias — Seeking information that confirms existing beliefs
  • Overconfidence — Overestimating your knowledge or abilities
  • Analysis Paralysis — Overthinking prevents action
  • Sunk Cost Fallacy — Continuing because of past investment, not future value
Practice Task: Apply the rational decision-making model to a real choice you're facing (e.g., choosing between job offers, courses, or purchases). List at least 4 criteria, weight them, evaluate 3 alternatives, and select the best. For group decision-making, practice the Nominal Group Technique with a team to solve a work problem.

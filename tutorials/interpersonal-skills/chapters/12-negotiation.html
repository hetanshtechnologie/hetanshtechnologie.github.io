12. Negotiation & Persuasion

Workplace & Social Skills

What is Negotiation?

Negotiation is a dialogue between two or more parties to reach a mutually acceptable agreement. It happens in everyday life — salary discussions, project deadlines, purchasing decisions, even where to eat dinner.

Distributive vs Integrative Negotiation

  • Distributive (Win-Lose) — Fixed pie; one party's gain is another's loss. Common in price negotiations.
  • Integrative (Win-Win) — Expand the pie; both parties can gain by finding creative solutions that satisfy both interests.

Key Negotiation Concepts

  • BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement. Your backup plan if negotiation fails. Knowing your BATNA gives you leverage.
  • Reservation Price — Your bottom line (walk-away point)
  • ZOPA — Zone Of Possible Agreement (overlap between both parties' acceptable ranges)
  • Anchoring — The first offer sets the reference point for negotiation

Negotiation Process

  1. Prepare — Know your goals, BATNA, and the other party's likely interests
  2. Discuss — Share perspectives, ask questions, build rapport
  3. Propose — Make your offer or counter-offer
  4. Bargain — Exchange concessions, explore trade-offs
  5. Agree — Confirm terms, document agreement

Persuasion Techniques

Robert Cialdini's six principles of persuasion:

  • Reciprocity — People feel obliged to return favors
  • Scarcity — Limited availability increases desirability
  • Authority — People follow credible experts
  • Consistency — People like to be consistent with past commitments
  • Liking — People are persuaded by those they like
  • Social Proof — People follow what others do

Ethics in Negotiation

Ethical negotiation builds long-term trust. Avoid lying, misrepresentation, or exploiting power imbalances. Transparent, principled negotiation (as taught by Harvard's "Getting to Yes") focuses on interests rather than positions and uses objective criteria.

Practice Task: Prepare for a hypothetical salary negotiation. Define your BATNA, reservation price, target, and anchoring offer. Identify at least 3 interests the employer might have (beyond salary). Practice the negotiation with a partner, focusing on integrative (win-win) solutions. Afterward, identify which of Cialdini's principles you used.

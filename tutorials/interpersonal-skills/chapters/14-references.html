Best Practices Cheat Sheet

  • Before speaking — Know your purpose, consider your audience, choose your medium
  • While listening — Be present, don't interrupt, paraphrase back
  • Giving feedback — Use SBI: Situation, Behavior, Impact
  • Receiving feedback — Listen, thank, reflect (don't defend)
  • In conflict — Use "I" statements, seek interests not positions, aim for win-win
  • In negotiation — Know your BATNA, expand the pie, use objective criteria
  • In teams — Clarify roles, communicate openly, celebrate wins

Q&A

Q: Can interpersonal skills really be learned?
A: Absolutely. While some people are naturally more outgoing, all interpersonal skills can be developed with deliberate practice, self-awareness, and feedback.

Q: What's the single most important interpersonal skill?
A: Active listening. Most communication problems stem from people not feeling heard. Listening builds trust, understanding, and rapport.

Q: How do I handle someone who won't listen?
A: Use "I" statements, ask if it's a good time to talk, find common ground, and if needed, involve a mediator or supervisor.

Q: How long does it take to improve interpersonal skills?
A: Small changes can show results immediately (e.g., paraphrasing). Significant transformation typically takes 3-6 months of consistent practice.

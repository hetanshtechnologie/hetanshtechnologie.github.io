10. Teamwork & Collaboration

Workplace & Social Skills

Why Teamwork Matters

Most professional work today is done in teams. Effective teamwork combines diverse skills, perspectives, and efforts toward a common goal. High-performing teams achieve more than the sum of their individual members.

Team Roles (Belbin Model)

Meredith Belbin identified nine team roles grouped into three categories:

  • Action-Oriented: Shaper (challenges), Implementer (executes), Completer-Finisher (details)
  • People-Oriented: Coordinator (clarifies goals), Teamworker (cooperates), Resource Investigator (networks)
  • Thought-Oriented: Plant (creative ideas), Monitor-Evaluator (analyzes), Specialist (expert knowledge)

Effective teams have a balance of these roles. No single role is better — diversity of roles strengthens the team.

Stages of Team Development (Tuckman)

  1. Forming — Team meets, establishes ground rules, polite and cautious
  2. Storming — Conflicts emerge, personalities clash, roles questioned
  3. Norming — Team establishes norms, builds trust, works cohesively
  4. Performing — Team operates at high efficiency, handles conflict constructively
  5. Adjourning — Project ends, team disbands, reflection occurs

Collaboration Tools & Best Practices

  • Set clear goals and define roles from the start
  • Communicate openly and frequently — share progress and blockers
  • Give and receive feedback constructively
  • Celebrate team wins and recognize individual contributions
  • Use collaboration tools (Slack, Teams, Trello, Notion) effectively
  • Respect diversity — different perspectives strengthen solutions

Customer Service Skills

Core principles: listen actively, empathize with the customer's situation, take ownership of the issue, communicate clearly, and follow through. The HEAT model: Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Take action.

Meetings That Work

Effective meetings have a clear agenda, start and end on time, include only necessary participants, stay focused, and end with action items. Virtual meeting tips: use video, mute when not speaking, use chat for questions, and share screens strategically.

Practice Task: In your next team meeting, observe which Belbin roles people naturally take. Notice which stages of Tuckman your team is in. Practice the HEAT model in a customer service scenario with a partner. Suggest one improvement to how your team runs meetings.

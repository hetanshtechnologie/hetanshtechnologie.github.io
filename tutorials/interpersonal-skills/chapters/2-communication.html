2. Communication Skills

Communication Skills

What is Communication?

Communication is the exchange of information, ideas, thoughts, and feelings between people through verbal and non-verbal methods. It is a two-way process involving a sender, a message, a medium, a receiver, and feedback.

Figure: The communication process — encoding, sending, decoding, and feedback with potential noise at each stage.

Principles of Effective Communication

  • Clarity — Be clear about your message and purpose
  • Conciseness — Keep it brief without losing key information
  • Correctness — Use accurate facts, grammar, and tone
  • Completeness — Include all necessary information
  • Consideration — Consider the receiver's perspective and feelings
  • Concreteness — Use specific facts and figures over vague statements
  • Courtesy — Show respect regardless of position

Common Communication Mistakes

  • Interrupting before the other person finishes
  • Assuming understanding without checking
  • Using jargon or overly complex language
  • Focusing on what to say next instead of listening
  • Letting emotions override logic
  • Multitasking during conversations

Feedback: Giving and Receiving

SBI Model for giving feedback: Situation (when/where), Behavior (what was observed), Impact (the effect). For receiving feedback, listen without defensiveness, ask clarifying questions, thank the giver, and reflect on the message.

Interview Skills

Preparation is key: research the company, practice common questions using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result), prepare questions to ask, and follow up with a thank-you note. For telephone/video interviews, ensure a quiet environment, good lighting, and test your technology beforehand.

Ladder of Inference

A mental model showing how we move from data to action: Observable Data → Selected Data → Added Meaning → Assumptions → Conclusions → Beliefs → Actions. The ladder helps us recognize when we're jumping to conclusions.

Practice Task: Practice the SBI feedback model with a partner. One person shares a scenario, the other gives feedback using Situation-Behavior-Impact. Then switch roles. For the Ladder of Inference, recall a time you made an incorrect assumption about someone and trace your thinking up the ladder.

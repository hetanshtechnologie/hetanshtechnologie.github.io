9. Conflict Resolution

Conflict Resolution

What is Conflict?

Conflict is a disagreement between two or more parties with opposing interests, values, or perspectives. It's natural and inevitable — but when handled well, conflict can lead to growth, innovation, and stronger relationships.

Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Styles

StyleAssertivenessCooperativenessWhen to Use
CompetingHighLowUrgent decisions, unpopular actions
CollaboratingHighHighComplex issues, need buy-in
CompromisingMediumMediumTime pressure, equal power
AvoidingLowLowTrivial matters, need to cool down
AccommodatingLowHighPreserving harmony, you're wrong

Steps for Constructive Conflict Resolution

  1. Create Safety — Choose a private, neutral setting. Set ground rules (no interrupting, no personal attacks)
  2. Share Perspectives — Each person describes their view without interruption. Use "I" statements
  3. Identify Interests — Ask "why" to surface underlying needs, not just positions
  4. Generate Options — Brainstorm solutions together without judgment
  5. Agree on Action — Choose a mutually acceptable solution with specific next steps
  6. Follow Up — Check in later to ensure the solution is working

Mediation

When parties cannot resolve conflict themselves, a neutral third-party mediator can help. The mediator doesn't decide — they facilitate communication, ensure fairness, and help parties find their own solution. Key mediator skills include neutrality, active listening, reframing, and patience.

De-escalation Techniques

  • Lower your voice when the other raises theirs
  • Acknowledge emotions: "I can see you're frustrated"
  • Use calming body language — open posture, palms visible
  • Take a break: "Let's pause and reconvene in 15 minutes"
  • Focus on common ground: "We both want this project to succeed"
Practice Task: Recall a recent conflict you were involved in. Analyze which conflict style was used by each party. How would the outcome differ with a different approach? Role-play a conflict scenario with a partner, practicing the 6-step resolution process. Practice using "I" statements: "I feel ___ when ___ because ___."

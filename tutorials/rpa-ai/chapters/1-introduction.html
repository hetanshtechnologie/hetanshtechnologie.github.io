1. Introduction to RPA & AI

Chapter 1

What is Automation?

Automation is using technology to perform tasks with reduced human intervention. Organizations have automated for centuries — from assembly lines to spreadsheets to email rules — but the current wave is different. Software can now replicate not just physical tasks but digital, cognitive, and even judgment tasks that previously required a human. This book is about the two biggest technologies driving that change: Robotic Process Automation (RPA) and Artificial Intelligence (AI).

RPA and AI are often discussed together under the umbrella term Intelligent Automation (IA). Understanding what each does alone, and how they combine, is the core theme of this book.

RPA vs AI vs Intelligent Automation

TechnologyWhat It DoesBest AtExample
RPAConfigures software "robots" (bots) to click, type, read, and move data between applications the way a human wouldRules-based, repetitive, high-volume digital tasksCopying data from a spreadsheet into an ERP form every morning
AIUses algorithms trained on data to recognize patterns, understand language, make predictions, and generate contentUnstructured, ambiguous, judgment tasksReading an invoice PDF and deciding which lines are line items
Intelligent Automation (RPA + AI)Combines RPA's execution with AI's perception and decision-makingEnd-to-end processes that mix structured and unstructured workA bot that reads emails, classifies them with NLP, and routes/acts on them
Simple rule of thumb: If you can write a step-by-step rule for the task, RPA alone can likely do it. If the task requires reading, understanding, or deciding in the face of ambiguity, you need AI — or AI wrapped by RPA.

The Business Case for Automation

Organizations invest in RPA and AI for a handful of measurable reasons:

  • Cost reduction: Bots are typically cheaper than human FTE for high-volume, rules-based work, operating 24x7.
  • Speed and throughput: A bot completes a data-entry task in minutes that takes a human tens of minutes; processes run around the clock.
  • Accuracy and consistency: Done right, bots do not make typos, skip steps, or get tired — every execution follows the same steps.
  • Employee experience: Automating the "swivel-chair" work (copying between systems) frees people for higher-value tasks, improving retention.
  • Scalability: You can run one bot on one machine or hundreds on a virtual fleet, scaling to spikes like month-end close.
  • Auditability: Every bot action can be logged, which supports compliance, audit, and continuous improvement.
Important nuance: Automation is not primarily about replacing people — the most successful programs are about augmenting them. Mature organizations describe the outcome as a "digital workforce" working alongside the human workforce.

Where RPA and AI Fit in the Organization

Automation is most valuable where it is aligned to business processes, not implemented in silos. Typical adoption patterns:

  • Finance: invoice processing, accounts payable/receivable, reconciliation, expense reporting.
  • HR: onboarding, payroll validation, timesheet processing, benefits administration.
  • Customer service: ticket routing, data lookup, form processing, chatbots.
  • IT and back office: password resets, user provisioning, data migration, report generation.
  • Supply chain: order entry, shipment tracking, inventory reconciliation, supplier onboarding.
  • Healthcare and insurance: claims processing, prior authorization, patient intake, document review.

The Digital Workforce Concept

Companies that scale automation think of bots as a workforce with the same disciplines as a human workforce: onboarding, training (development), supervision (monitoring), performance management (metrics and SLAs), and offboarding (decommissioning). A Center of Excellence (CoE) provides the governance, standards, and shared infrastructure to run this workforce. We cover the CoE in depth in Chapter 5.

The Evolution Toward Intelligent Automation

The automation industry has moved through distinct generations:

  • Gen 1 — Task automation: Simple macros and scripting (Excel macros, shell scripts). Fragile, hard to maintain.
  • Gen 2 — RPA: Robust UI-level automation with selectors, orchestrators, and enterprise governance. Great for stable, rules-based processes.
  • Gen 3 — RPA + AI (Intelligent Automation): RPA orchestrates and executes; AI provides OCR, NLP, vision, and prediction. Bots can now handle semi-structured documents, emails, and conversations.
  • Gen 4 — Agentic AI / GenAI-driven: Large language models and AI agents can plan, reason, and execute multi-step work, sometimes generating the automation itself. This is the frontier this book will also explore.

3. RPA Tools & Platforms

Chapter 3

The RPA Market Landscape

The RPA market is dominated by a few large vendors plus a long tail of niche and open-source tools. The leading platforms are UiPath, Automation Anywhere, and Microsoft Power Automate, with Blue Prism (now part of SS&C) and WorkFusion in the enterprise space, and Robocorp, TagUI, and OpenRPA offering open-source options.

Choosing a platform is one of the most consequential decisions in an automation program. The "best" tool depends on your existing stack, licensing model, IT environment, and use cases — not on marketing rankings.

Tool Anatomy: What to Look For

Regardless of vendor, an enterprise RPA platform has a consistent set of components. When evaluating tools, check each area:

  • Studio / Design tool: visual workflow designer, recorders, selectors, debugger, version control.
  • Orchestrator / Control Room: schedules bots, manages credentials and queues, monitors runs, handles license allocation.
  • Runner / Bot runtime: executes workflows on desktops or servers (attended/unattended).
  • Connectors and activities library: prebuilt integrations (Excel, databases, email, browsers, SAP, etc.).
  • Credential vault: secure storage for passwords and secrets with role-based access.
  • Queues and human-in-the-loop: task queues for exceptions and approval workflows.
  • AI services: OCR, document understanding, NLP, and ML model hosting (varies by vendor).
  • Governance & analytics: logging, dashboards, audit trails, capacity and cost reporting.

Licensing Models

RPA licensing is a major cost consideration. Common models include:

ModelWhat You Pay ForTypical Cost Driver
Bot / Runtime licenseNumber of bot runners (machines that execute bots)Fleet size — expensive at scale unless you optimize usage
Per-user (attended)Number of human users who use attended botsAgent-facing automation, e.g., call centers
Per-flow / capacity unitsConsumption-based, e.g., Power Automate flow runsVolume of executions; predictable at high volume
Cloud vs on-premSaaS subscription vs perpetual license + infrastructureOps overhead and security requirements
Licensing caution: Some vendors count an unattended "bot" per machine/runner even when idle. Design your deployment to share runners across queues and time slots — a common source of overspending in immature programs.

Open-Source and Code-First Options

Open-source RPA is viable for developers and for teams with constrained budgets:

  • Robocorp: Python-based, code-first, with a free open-source robot framework and a commercial cloud orchestrator.
  • OpenRPA: an open-source .NET tool with connectors, popular for small to mid-scale automation.
  • TagUI: a lightweight, script-based UI automation tool with a simple language.
  • Browser-level automation: Selenium, Playwright, and Puppeteer automate web UIs with full programmatic control — the "developer's RPA".

The trade-off: open-source and code-first tools give you flexibility and lower cost but require engineering skills, and you must assemble governance, scheduling, and monitoring yourself.

Evaluating and Selecting a Tool

A structured selection process prevents vendor-lock-in regret:

  1. Define requirements: use cases, attended vs unattended mix, environment (Windows, Citrix, cloud, web), integration needs, compliance constraints, and budget.
  2. Shortlist 2-3 platforms: include one you already use if you are on Microsoft 365 (Power Automate).
  3. Run a proof of concept (PoC): build the same two pilot processes on each candidate on identical data.
  4. Score objectively: weigh dev speed, reliability, scalability, licensing cost at target volume, AI features, and support.
  5. Check the ecosystem: community size, marketplace activities, training content, and partner availability.

    6. Case Study: Choosing Power Automate in a Microsoft Shop

    A mid-size finance team on Microsoft 365 compared UiPath and Power Automate for invoice data entry. Their requirements: attended assistance for agents, integration with SharePoint and Outlook, and low developer overhead. Power Automate won because the Finance team could build most flows with minimal IT help, licensing rode existing M365 agreements, and the audit trail met their needs. They reserved UiPath for a later phase involving Citrix-based legacy systems where Power Automate's UI flows were weaker.

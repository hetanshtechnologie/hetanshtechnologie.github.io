8. AI-Powered Automation Use Cases

Chapter 8

A Pattern Library, Not a Feature List

The most valuable way to learn IA is to recognize the recurring patterns behind specific use cases. Almost every intelligent automation you encounter is one of these patterns:

  • Read → Structure → Enter: extract data from documents/emails, then enter it into systems.
  • Classify → Route: understand a message or document and route it to the right team or action.
  • Score → Decide → Escalate: predict risk or quality, then act or escalate by confidence.
  • Listen → Draft → Respond: understand incoming communication and generate an appropriate response.
  • Monitor → Alert → Act: watch signals, detect anomalies, and trigger follow-up actions.

Finance: Invoice and Expense Automation

Accounts payable is the most famous IA use case. The pattern Read → Structure → Enter applies:

  • Document AI extracts vendor, dates, PO numbers, and line items from invoices in any layout.
  • Rules validate against the PO and vendor master; duplicates are detected.
  • RPA enters approved invoices into the ERP and schedules payments.
  • Expense reports get the same treatment: receipts are read, categorized, and matched to credit-card feeds.
  • Fraud flags: ML scores unusual patterns (round amounts, same vendor frequency) and routes them to review.

Customer Service: Triage, Data Lookup, and Draft Replies

The Classify → Route and Listen → Draft → Respond patterns transform contact centers:

  • NLP classifies each incoming ticket/email by intent, urgency, and sentiment.
  • RPA retrieves the customer's account, order history, and policies across systems.
  • An LLM drafts a personalized response grounded in the retrieved context (RAG); the agent reviews and sends.
  • Fully automated responses handle the routine cases (order status, reset links) with a confidence gate.
  • Voice channels use a virtual agent for the first line and transfer to humans with full context.
Impact story: A telecom's contact center cut average handling time 40% by having bots assemble customer context before the agent connects, and by auto-answering the ~25% of inquiries that were routine. Agents now spend their time on complex issues.

Insurance and Healthcare: Claims and Intake

Document-heavy industries run almost entirely on Read → Structure → Enter plus scoring:

  • Claims intake: documents (claims forms, medical reports, photos) are classified, read, and fields extracted.
  • Adjudication support: ML scores claim complexity and risk; simple claims auto-approve within policy, complex ones route to adjusters with extracted evidence.
  • Prior authorization in healthcare: documents are processed, coding validated against payer policy, and submissions generated.
  • Fraud detection: anomaly models flag suspicious claim patterns for investigation.

HR and Back Office: Onboarding and Data Stewardship

  • Onboarding: bot collects documents from a portal, verifies identity documents with document AI, creates accounts, and schedules training.
  • Payroll and timesheets: attendance records are read from varied formats, validated, and loaded; exceptions (missing clock-outs) are flagged.
  • Data quality: bots deduplicate records, normalize addresses, and reconcile data between systems — classic RPA with light AI for fuzzy matching.
  • Resume screening support: NLP extracts skills and experience from resumes; the bot scores candidates against the job profile and routes shortlists for human review.
Fairness caution: Any people-facing AI (screening, scoring) must be reviewed for bias and must keep a human decision-maker with appeal rights. Regulated jurisdictions impose specific rules; treat "AI decides about people" as high-risk by default.

Supply Chain and Operations: Orders, Tracking, Reconciliation

  • Order entry from emails, EDI variants, and portals is read and entered (Read → Structure → Enter).
  • Shipment tracking: bots poll carriers, parse tracking statuses (including PDFs and images), and update systems.
  • Inventory reconciliation: cross-system data is compared; mismatches trigger investigation tickets.
  • Forecasting: ML predicts demand and safety stock; bots apply the numbers across planning systems.
  • Supplier onboarding: documents (W-9/TAX, bank details) are read, validated, and entered with risk scoring.

IT and Security Operations

  • Password resets and access requests: bots handle the routine 80% with identity-system APIs.
  • Ticket enrichment and triage: NLP classifies and routes incidents; bots gather logs and configs automatically.
  • Alert triage in security: SOAR-style playbooks enrich alerts with threat intel and take containment actions — the automation chapter of the security world.
  • IT change and release notes: LLMs summarize changes and draft communications.

Choosing and Prioritizing Use Cases

When evaluating any use case, score it on:

DimensionAsk
Volume and frequencyIs it executed often enough to matter?
Data availabilityIs the input accessible (digital) and labeled/trainable?
Rule clarity vs ambiguityMostly rules → RPA; mostly ambiguity → need AI
System accessAPIs available, or UI automation needed?
Risk and exceptionsCan failures escalate safely? Cost of error?
ComplianceAudit, privacy, and approval requirements
Measureable valueCan you quantify time, cost, error, or experience improvement?

Case Study: Multi-Pattern Customer Onboarding

A bank's new-customer onboarding combined every pattern: documents uploaded by the customer were classified and read (document AI), KYC data validated against watchlists (rules + scoring), accounts provisioned via APIs (RPA), status communications drafted by an LLM and approved by staff (draft + approve), and applications below confidence threshold routed to a specialist queue (escalation). Straight-through rate reached ~70%, onboarding time dropped from days to hours, and the audit trail covered every decision.

9. Document Processing & OCR

Chapter 9

The Document Problem

A large share of business work happens in documents: invoices, contracts, claims, IDs, forms, and reports. Much of it is scanned or photographed (unstructured), and layouts vary wildly between vendors and versions. Documents are the single biggest reason classic RPA alone is not enough — and the most common gateway into AI for automation teams.

OCR vs Intelligent Document Processing (IDP)

These terms are often conflated. They are different layers of the same stack:

LayerWhat It DoesOutput
OCR (raw text recognition)Converts pixels into charactersPlain text, sometimes with bounding boxes
Layout understandingDetects tables, headers, sections, and reading orderStructured page structure
ClassificationDecides what kind of document it isDocument type label (invoice, contract, ID...)
ExtractionPulls specific fields out (vendor, date, total)Structured key-value data
Validation and enrichmentChecks extracted data against rules and reference dataValidated records with confidence scores

Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) is the whole stack — OCR plus layout, classification, extraction, and validation — packaged so an RPA bot can consume it as a service.

The IDP Pipeline in an Automation Workflow

  1. Ingest: the bot collects documents from email, scan folder, portal upload, or shared drive.
  2. Preprocess: deskew, denoise, crop, and normalize images to improve OCR accuracy.
  3. Classify: determine the document type (needed when a folder contains mixed documents).
  4. OCR: convert to text; modern engines preserve layout and give per-word confidence and coordinates.
  5. Extract: map text/fields into structured data, either with template-based rules or with ML/LLM extraction.
  6. Validate: apply business rules (date ranges, amount formats, cross-field consistency).
  7. Escalate or continue: low confidence or failed validation goes to a human review queue; the rest flows into the RPA action step.

Document Types and Their Challenges

Document TypeChallengeTechnique
Machine-printed forms (uniform layout)Easy but high volumeTemplate-based extraction + OCR

Scanned/photographed documentsPoor quality, skew, noiseImage preprocessing + robust OCR
Handwritten contentHigh variability, low accuracySpecialized handwriting models + human review

Tables in documentsMerged cells, spanning, no gridLayout-aware table extraction

Extraction Approaches: Rules, ML, and LLMs

Choosing the extraction engine is a key decision:

  • Template/keyword rules: fastest and cheapest for fixed layouts, but brittle — any layout change breaks it.
  • Classic ML (custom models): robust across layouts for a specific field set; requires labeled training data and retraining on drift.
  • Vendor IDP services: UiPath Document Understanding, Azure Form/Document Intelligence, AWS Textract, Google Document AI — prebuilt models for common documents (invoices, receipts, IDs) plus custom models.
  • LLM-based extraction: prompt a large model with the OCR text or images and ask for structured JSON. Extremely flexible, strong with varied layouts and unusual fields; needs validation and cost/accuracy monitoring.
Modern practice: Teams increasingly use an LLM for extraction with a strong system prompt and a few examples, then a rule-based validator on the output, then a confidence gate to a human reviewer. This gets 90%+ of the value of custom ML with a fraction of the training data effort.

Measuring Extraction Quality

Extraction must be measured before and after launch:

  • Field accuracy: % of extracted values correct on a labeled sample.
  • Precision / recall per field: misses vs wrong values, weighted by business impact.
  • Confidence calibration: does a 0.95 confidence really mean ~95% correct?
  • Human correction rate: how often reviewers change extracted values.
  • Review throughput: how fast the human queue can clear.
Baseline first: measure accuracy on a representative sample before building anything. If OCR on your document quality is poor, no extraction technique will rescue it — fix image capture quality first (scan resolution, lighting, format).

Case Study: Scaling Invoice IDP

An AP team received 5,000 invoices/month in 40+ vendor layouts, many scanned. They deployed an IDP pipeline: image preprocessing → vendor OCR → LLM extraction → business-rule validation → confidence gate. Results: 86% straight-through with field accuracy above 97% on validated samples; the remaining 14% reached the AP queue pre-filled with extracted data and a reason. When a new vendor appeared, only the prompt/examples needed a small update, not a retrained model.

Pitfalls in Document Automation

  • Garbage-in: bad scans, rotated pages, and missing pages cripple accuracy.
  • Silent misreads: OCR errors that pass validation (e.g., $1,000 vs $10,000) — design validators that catch impossible values.
  • Layout overfitting: templates tuned to one vendor fail on the next.
  • No human path: low-confidence documents pile up invisibly.
  • Privacy leakage: documents contain personal data; ensure the pipeline (including cloud AI) complies with data residency and retention rules.
Practice Task: Collect 10 real documents you can legally use (invoices, forms, receipts). Using any free OCR (Tesseract, a cloud trial, or your RPA tool's trial) and your preferred method (template, vendor IDP, or an LLM), extract 5 key fields from each. Record accuracy per field, note which documents failed and why, and design the validation rules you would add. Document your results.

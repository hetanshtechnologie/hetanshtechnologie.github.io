7. Intelligent Automation: RPA + AI

Chapter 7

Why Combine RPA and AI?

RPA alone automates the structured parts of a process. Most real processes are not purely structured — they contain emails to read, documents to interpret, exceptions to judge, and conversations to understand. That is where AI comes in. Intelligent Automation (IA) is the discipline of combining robotic execution with AI capabilities so that a bot can not only do the work but also understand the work.

Without AIWith AI (Intelligent Automation)
Bot processes a CSV of clean, structured ordersBot reads orders from emails, extracts them from attachments, and routes exceptions
Bot enters data into a form with a fixed layoutBot reads varied invoice layouts and enters the extracted fields
Bot sends the same canned response to every ticketBot understands the ticket intent and drafts a tailored, validated response
Bot flags rows by a fixed threshold ruleBot scores each case with a model and escalates high-risk ones

An Intelligent Automation Reference Architecture

A production IA solution layers AI around a core RPA workflow:

  1. Trigger: email, schedule, queue, event, or user action.
  2. Ingest and understand (AI): OCR, document AI, NLP, LLM — read and interpret unstructured input.
  3. Decide (AI + rules): classification, scoring, validation, confidence gating.
  4. Act (RPA + APIs): enter data, update systems, generate outputs, send communications.
  5. Verify and learn: validate outcomes, capture human corrections, feed back to improve models/prompts.
Mental model: RPA is the arms and legs; AI is the eyes and brain; the orchestrator is the nervous system that ties them together; humans are the supervisors who handle what the system cannot.

Integration Patterns

There are several recurring ways RPA and AI are wired together:

  • AI in front of RPA: AI understands and structures input, then hands clean data to the bot to execute. Example: document AI extracts invoice fields, bot enters them into the ERP.
  • AI inside RPA: the bot calls an AI service mid-workflow for a specific decision or extraction. Example: bot asks a sentiment model how to route a response.
  • RPA in front of AI: the bot orchestrates fetching and assembling context for an AI service, then acts on the result. Example: bot retrieves customer records, sends them to an LLM to draft a summary, and saves it.
  • AI agents calling RPA: an agentic AI orchestrator decides which automations to invoke based on intent (the frontier pattern — Chapter 12).

Human-in-the-Loop Design

Even the most intelligent automation needs a human channel. Good designs make escalation first-class:

  • Confidence thresholds: AI results below a confidence score route to a human queue.
  • Exception queues: work items the bot cannot process land in a task queue with full context.
  • Approval workflows: irreversible or high-value actions require human sign-off.
  • Correction loops: human corrections feed back to retrain models or refine prompts — the "human in the loop trains the machine".
Design rule: An exception should never vanish. Every item a bot fails must appear somewhere a human can see, act on, and close — with a reason recorded. A bot that silently drops work is worse than no bot at all.

Example: End-to-End Invoice Processing

The canonical IA use case shows every pattern at once:

  1. Trigger: vendor emails an invoice PDF to a shared inbox.
  2. Understand: the email is read; the attachment is classified as an invoice; document AI extracts vendor, date, line items, and total (even with varied layouts).
  3. Decide: rules validate the invoice (PO match, amounts, duplicates); an ML model flags risk; a confidence score is computed.
  4. Act: RPA enters the data into the ERP, attaches the original, updates the PO status, and posts a payment-scheduling record.
  5. Escalate: mismatched or low-confidence invoices route to the AP team's queue with the extracted data pre-filled and a reason listed.
  6. Learn: corrections from AP are captured to improve extraction and validation.

Result: 80%+ of invoices process straight through; the AP team handles only exceptions; every step is logged and auditable.

Value Measurement for IA

Beyond straight-through rate, measure:

  • Straight-through processing rate: % of items fully handled with no human touch.
  • Accuracy: extraction/classification accuracy on validated samples.
  • Escalation rate and reasons: what keeps items from straight-through.
  • Cycle time: end-to-end time for automated vs manual handling.
  • Cost per transaction: fully-loaded cost of the automated path.
  • Correction rate: how often human reviews change the AI's answer.

Pitfalls in Combining RPA + AI

  • AI slop without gates: feeding raw model output straight into production actions without validation.
  • Over-engineering: using an LLM where a regex or a rule would be more reliable, cheaper, and auditable.
  • Shadow AI: AI features enabled by developers without security, privacy, or governance review.
  • Missing fallbacks: no human path when the model or the bot fails.
  • Cost surprises: per-token LLM costs at scale can exceed expectations; measure and budget.
Engineering discipline: Use the simplest reliable approach (rules → classic ML → LLM), validate every AI output, log every decision, and keep a human path for everything that matters. IA is not "replace people" — it is "escalate intelligently".

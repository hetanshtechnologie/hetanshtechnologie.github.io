2. Project Management Methodologies

Methodologies

Agile Methodology

Agile focuses on iterative development, collaboration, and responding to change. Key principles from the Agile Manifesto:

  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  • Working software over comprehensive documentation
  • Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
  • Responding to change over following a plan

Popular Agile frameworks include Scrum (sprints, daily standups, retrospectives) and Kanban (visual boards, WIP limits, continuous delivery).

Case Study: Spotify uses Agile with Squads (small cross-functional teams), Tribes (groups of squads), and Chapters/Guilds for cross-team coordination. This structure enables rapid feature releases while maintaining alignment.

PRINCE2

PRINCE2 (Projects IN Controlled Environments) is a process-based method with seven principles, seven themes, and seven processes. Key features:

  • Business Case driven — continuous justification
  • Defined roles and responsibilities
  • Management by stages
  • Management by exception
  • Focus on products, not activities

Traditional (Waterfall) PM

Linear, sequential approach with distinct phases: Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring → Closure. Best suited for projects with clear requirements and low uncertainty.

Extreme Project Management (XPM)

For high-uncertainty, high-speed projects. Embraces change, uses lightweight documentation, and relies heavily on team communication and stakeholder involvement.

Process-Based Management

Focuses on optimizing business processes rather than individual tasks. Uses techniques like Business Process Reengineering (BPR) and Six Sigma to improve efficiency and quality.

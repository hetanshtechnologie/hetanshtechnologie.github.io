The Myth of Multitasking

Introduction — We live in a world that glorifies multitasking. Job descriptions demand it, people brag about it, and technology encourages it. But here’s the truth: multitasking is a myth. Your brain isn’t wired to do multiple high value tasks at once. What you’re really doing is switching rapidly between tasks, and every switch costs you focus, energy, and efficiency.

Story / Analogy — Imagine juggling balls. With two, you can manage. Add a third, and it gets tricky. Add five, and you drop them all. That’s multitasking in your daily life — the more you juggle, the more you drop.

Practical Steps to Break the Habit
  1. Single Task Commitment — Choose one task and commit until completion.
  2. Batch Similar Work — Group emails, calls, or admin tasks together.
  3. Protect Deep Work — Block 2–3 hours for high value tasks.
  4. Audit Your Day — Did I finish one meaningful task, or just touch many?
Reflection Exercise
Write down three tasks you often try to do simultaneously. Circle the one that truly matters. Commit tomorrow to doing it alone, without switching, until it’s complete.

Multitasking is not a badge of honor — it’s a trap. True productivity comes from deep focus on one task at a time. When you stop juggling and start concentrating, you’ll achieve more in less time, with less stress.

2. Product Strategy & Roadmap

Strategy & Roadmap

Develop and Execute a Clear Product Vision

A clear product vision defines what the product will become and the value it will deliver. The Senior Product Owner must develop and execute a product vision, strategy, and roadmap aligned with organizational objectives and digital transformation goals. A strong vision answers three questions:

  • Who — Which customers, businesses, and users are we serving?
  • What — What problems do we solve, and what outcomes do we create?
  • Why — Why does this matter to the organization and its customers?
Vision Statement Template: "We enable [customers/businesses] to [achieve outcome] by providing [product capability], ensuring [differentiator such as security, speed, or reliability]."

Driving the Evolution of Payment Solutions

Payment products must evolve to meet changing customer, business, and regulatory requirements. Evolution is driven by:

  • Customer behavior — Shift to real-time, mobile, and API-based payments.
  • Regulatory mandates — New standards such as ISO 20022 migration, open banking, and instant payment rails.
  • Business strategy — New markets, new product lines, and M&A integration.
  • Technology — Cloud, AI/ML, blockchain, and tokenization opportunities.

Promoting Standardization and Consistency

A key responsibility is promoting standardization and consistency in payment processing and operating models across markets and business units wherever feasible. Standardization reduces complexity, cost, and risk:

BenefitDescription
Cost ReductionOne platform instead of many bespoke implementations
Faster DeliveryReusable capabilities and shared components
Risk ReductionConsistent controls, monitoring, and operational practices
ScalabilityEasier to onboard new markets and products
ComplianceConsistent regulatory and audit responses

Prioritizing Product Investments

Prioritization decides what gets built first. The Senior Product Owner prioritizes based on customer needs, business value, operational benefits, and risk considerations. Common prioritization frameworks include:

  • MoSCoW — Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have.
  • RICE — Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort.
  • Value vs. Effort Matrix — Plot initiatives by business value and delivery effort.
  • Kano Model — Distinguish basic, performance, and delight features.
Example: A payment platform must choose between building an instant-payment notification feature (high customer value) and a regulatory reporting upgrade (mandatory). The Senior Product Owner prioritizes the regulatory change first because it is a compliance obligation, then sequences the notification feature in the next release.

Identifying Improvement Opportunities

Continuous improvement focuses on customer experience, operational efficiency, automation, and straight-through processing (STP). STP refers to processing payments automatically from initiation to settlement without manual intervention. Opportunities include:

  • Automating exception handling and repair queues
  • Reducing manual data entry through data enrichment
  • Enhancing real-time monitoring and alerting
  • Improving self-service and customer notification channels
  • Simplifying the product to reduce user friction

Building the Roadmap

A roadmap is a prioritized plan of initiatives over time. Effective roadmaps are:

  1. Outcome-oriented — Focus on value delivered, not just features shipped.
  2. Time-boxed — Organized into quarters or release windows.
  3. Transparent — Visible to stakeholders with clear status.
  4. Flexible — Able to absorb new priorities as conditions change.
  5. Aligned — Connected to organizational strategy and OKRs.
Practice Task: For a payment app, create a quarterly roadmap with 6 initiatives. Score each using RICE and rank them. Identify which are regulatory mandates, which are customer-experience wins, and which are operational-efficiency improvements.

11. Payments & Financial Messaging

Domain Knowledge

The Payments Landscape

Payments move money between parties and are the backbone of commerce and banking. The payments ecosystem includes:

  • Payers and payees — Individuals, businesses, governments
  • Banks and financial institutions — Issuers, acquirers, correspondents
  • Payment schemes — Visa, Mastercard, UPI, RTGS, NEFT, SWIFT, SEPA
  • Fintechs and processors — Payment gateways, wallets, neobanks
  • Regulators — Central banks and supervisory authorities

Transaction Banking

Transaction banking covers the payments, cash management, and trade finance services banks provide to corporate clients. Core components:

ComponentDescription
PaymentsDomestic and cross-border payment execution
Cash ManagementLiquidity visibility, sweeping, pooling, account services
Trade FinanceLetters of credit, guarantees, supply chain finance
Liquidity ManagementOptimizing cash positions across accounts and currencies

Payment Processing Lifecycle

  1. Initiation — Customer or client initiates a payment
  2. Validation — Format, limit, and account checks
  3. Sanction/AML screening — Compliance checks against watchlists
  4. Authorization — Funds availability and approval
  5. Clearing — Exchange and confirmation between banks
  6. Settlement — Final transfer of funds
  7. Notification — Advices and statements to parties
  8. Reconciliation — Matching internal records with statements

SWIFT

SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is the network that banks use to exchange financial messages securely. Key points:

  • Not a payment system — it is a messaging network
  • Settlement happens through correspondent banking and nostro/vostro accounts
  • Traditional MT messages (e.g., MT103 for customer transfers)
  • Migration to MX (ISO 20022) messages underway
  • More than 11,000 institutions connected worldwide
Common SWIFT MT Messages: MT103 (customer credit transfer), MT202 (bank transfer), MT199/MT299 (free format), MT940/MT950 (statement messages).

ISO 20022

ISO 20022 is a global standard for financial messaging, using structured XML/JSON formats with rich, machine-readable data. Benefits:

  • Richer data — More fields, structured remittance information
  • Interoperability — Common language across systems and networks
  • Automation — Higher STP rates and fewer repairs
  • Regulatory reporting — Easier data extraction for compliance
  • Future-proofing — Designed for evolving business needs

Cash Management Concepts

  • Notional pooling — Aggregating balances for interest calculation without physical movement
  • Zero balancing — Sweeping balances to a concentration account
  • Multi-currency accounts — Managing several currencies under one account
  • Liquidity forecasting — Predicting cash positions for planning

Regulatory Considerations

  • AML/KYC — Anti-money laundering and know-your-customer requirements
  • Sanctions screening — Checking parties against sanctions lists
  • Cross-border reporting — Regulatory reporting of international payments
  • Data protection — Handling of personal and financial data
Example: As ISO 20022 migration approaches, a bank's Senior Product Owner must ensure the payment platform can send and receive MX messages, map MT fields to MX fields, and handle richer remittance data. The change affects technology, operations, and reporting — requiring cross-functional coordination.
Practice Task: Research one payment rail (e.g., UPI, SEPA Instant, FedNow) and document its features, participants, settlement model, and data requirements. Explain how ISO 20022 relates to it.

6. Regulatory, Risk & Compliance Management

Governance

The Compliance Mandate

The Senior Product Owner ensures all regulatory, mandatory, and business-driven changes are appropriately defined, prioritized, developed, tested, and implemented. In payments and financial services, regulatory change is constant and non-negotiable.

Partnering with Risk, Compliance, Legal & Governance

Strong partnerships ensure adherence to applicable regulations, policies, and standards. The Product Owner works with:

TeamTypical Focus
RiskFraud prevention, sanctions screening, risk appetite, loss control
ComplianceRegulatory obligations, AML/KYC, reporting, licensing
LegalContracts, customer terms, data protection
GovernanceChange approval, audit readiness, policy adherence

Embedding Controls into Product Changes

Product changes must incorporate appropriate controls, monitoring, reporting, and risk mitigation measures. Controls fall into categories:

  • Preventive controls — Stop issues before they occur (e.g., validation rules, limits, screening)
  • Detective controls — Identify issues when they occur (e.g., monitoring, alerts, reports)
  • Corrective controls — Remediate and learn from issues (e.g., incident response, root cause analysis)

Regulatory Change Management Process

  1. Identify — Monitor regulatory developments relevant to payments
  2. Assess — Determine impact on products, processes, and customers
  3. Plan — Define required changes and delivery timeline
  4. Implement — Develop, test, and deploy changes
  5. Validate — Confirm compliance and effectiveness
  6. Monitor — Track ongoing adherence and reporting
Example Regulatory Drivers in Payments: RBI payment system directions, PSD2/open banking in Europe, ISO 20022 migration, FATF recommendations for AML, local data protection laws (e.g., DPDP Act in India, GDPR in Europe).

Maintaining Industry Awareness

The Senior Product Owner maintains awareness of industry trends, regulatory developments, and market practices relevant to payments and financial services. Sources include:

  • Regulator publications and consultations (RBI, PRA, ECB, Fed)
  • Industry bodies (SWIFT, ISO, NPCI, EPC)
  • Analyst reports (McKinsey, BCG, Gartner)
  • Conferences and working groups
  • Competitor and fintech monitoring

Example: A new regulatory reporting requirement is issued with a 6-month deadline. The Senior Product Owner sequences it ahead of non-mandatory backlog items, engages compliance early to define the data requirements, and runs parallel testing with the regulator's test environment to ensure readiness.
Practice Task: Choose a recent regulatory change in payments (e.g., ISO 20022 migration or new AML rules). Draft an impact assessment covering affected products, processes, customers, and the controls required. Propose a delivery plan with milestones.

12. Agile, Scrum & SAFe Delivery

Delivery Frameworks

Agile Principles

Agile is a philosophy based on the Agile Manifesto. Its four values:

  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  • Working software over comprehensive documentation
  • Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
  • Responding to change over following a plan

Scrum Framework

Scrum is the most widely used Agile framework. It defines roles, events, and artifacts:

Roles

RoleResponsibility
Product OwnerOwns the backlog, maximizes value, prioritizes
Scrum MasterFacilitates the process, removes impediments
Development TeamBuilds and tests the product

Events

  • Sprint — Time-boxed iteration (typically 1-4 weeks)
  • Sprint Planning — Agree what will be delivered in the sprint
  • Daily Scrum — 15-minute daily coordination
  • Sprint Review — Demonstrate completed work to stakeholders
  • Sprint Retrospective — Inspect and adapt the process

Artifacts

  • Product Backlog — Ordered list of everything the product needs
  • Sprint Backlog — Selected work for the current sprint
  • Increment — Usable, potentially releasable product at sprint end

The Product Owner in Scrum

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and managing the product backlog:

  • Defining and clearly communicating product goal
  • Creating and ordering backlog items
  • Optimizing value of the development team's work
  • Ensuring the backlog is visible, transparent, and understood
  • Deciding what gets released

SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework)

SAFe scales Agile to the enterprise level for multiple teams working on a common solution. Key elements:

ElementDescription
Program Increment (PI)8-12 week planning and execution cycle
Agile Release Train (ART)Long-lived team of Agile teams delivering together
PI PlanningMulti-team planning event at the start of each PI
Product ManagementOwns program backlog and solution roadmap
Solution TrainCoordinates multiple ARTs for large solutions
SAFe POPM: The SAFe Product Owner/Product Manager certification covers both roles and is valuable for enterprise product professionals.

Defining Ready and Done

  • Definition of Ready — Backlog items meet a standard before a team starts them (clear, sized, dependencies identified, acceptance criteria defined).
  • Definition of Done — Work meets a standard before it is considered complete (tested, documented, deployable, accepted).

Common Product Owner Mistakes

  • Acting as a secretary, writing stories without ownership
  • Ignoring technical debt and enablers
  • Not saying no, and letting the backlog balloon
  • Delegating prioritization to stakeholders
  • Missing ceremonies and losing visibility
Example: In a SAFe ART delivering a payments platform, the Senior Product Owner participates in PI planning, coordinates dependencies with the operations and compliance teams, and aligns the program backlog with the regulatory calendar. This ensures mandatory changes are sequenced alongside business features.
Practice Task: For your product, write a Definition of Ready and Definition of Done. Practice writing 10 backlog items and ordering them. Describe how you would run a sprint review for stakeholders.

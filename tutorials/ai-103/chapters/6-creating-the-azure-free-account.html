Creating the Azure Free Account

Everything you build in this course runs on Microsoft Azure, and the cheapest way to get started is the Azure free account. It gives you a real subscription with real resources at no initial cost — exactly what you need to follow along without worrying about a bill. This chapter walks you through what the free account includes, how to create it, and how to stay safely within its limits.

What the Free Account Includes

The Azure free account is not a time-limited trial that disappears. It is a permanent account with a set of free allowances that refresh each month:

  • A starting credit: A one-time amount, typically enough to experiment with paid services for a few weeks.
  • Free services: A curated list of always-free services, including many AI building blocks, each with monthly usage caps.
  • Access to every Azure service: You can provision paid services too; you are simply billed for whatever exceeds the free allowances.
  • No charge for the account itself: Creating and keeping the account costs nothing.

Details such as the exact credit amount and the free-services list change from time to time and vary by region. Always confirm the current terms on the Azure pricing page before assuming what is free. The general structure — one-time credit plus monthly free allowances — has been stable for years.

Requirements Before You Start

Creating the account is quick, but you need a few things ready:

  • A working email address you can verify.
  • A valid payment card. The card is for identity verification and to bill you if you ever exceed free limits — you are not charged to create the account, and many regions only place a small temporary authorization.
  • A phone number for verification.
  • Microsoft account credentials, or the willingness to create one during sign-up.

If you already have an Azure subscription through work or school, you can use it for this course instead. Just be aware that corporate subscriptions often have policies, spending controls, or approval workflows that a personal free account does not. For a clean learning environment, a fresh personal free account is usually easier.

Step-by-Step: Create the Free Account

Exercise 1: Sign up for an Azure free account

  1. Open a browser and go to the Azure free account sign-up page at https://azure.microsoft.com/free.
  2. Click Start free. If prompted, sign in with your Microsoft account or create one. A Microsoft account is any email address — you can register a new one here if you prefer to keep your Azure identity separate.
  3. Enter your personal details: country or region, name, and contact information.
  4. Verify your identity by phone. Microsoft texts or calls you with a code; enter the code to continue.
  5. Add a credit or debit card for identity verification. Confirm you have read and agreed to the subscription agreement.
  6. Click Sign up. Azure provisions your free subscription, which usually takes under a minute.
  7. Once the sign-up finishes, open the Azure portal at https://portal.azure.com. You should see a subscription named something like "Azure subscription 1" with your free credit applied.

Write down the account you used, because the Azure portal, the Foundry portal, and the Azure CLI all sign in with the same Microsoft account. Keeping one account for all your cloud learning avoids the confusing "wrong tenant" and "no subscription found" errors that plague developers juggling multiple identities.

Verify Your Subscription

Before building anything, confirm the account is healthy. From the Azure portal:

  • Click the menu and open Subscriptions. You should see your free subscription with a state of Enabled.
  • Open Cost Management + Billing to view your credit balance and any usage so far.
  • Set a budget alert: create a budget and a notification so Azure emails you if your spending approaches the limit. This is the single best habit for a free account.

Your free credit and allowances are generous, but they are not infinite. The two ways students accidentally incur charges are leaving GPU-backed model deployments running and letting batch jobs run unchecked. Both are avoidable by deleting resources you are not actively using and by always checking a model's pricing tier before deploying.

Using the Free Account with Foundry

Your free subscription is fully compatible with Microsoft Foundry. The resource you created in the earlier lab lives inside this subscription, and the free tier covers most of the development work in this course. When a chapter asks you to deploy a model, choose the free or standard deployment type and keep the traffic low — the usage caps are designed for exactly this kind of learning workload.

Upgrading Later

When you outgrow the free account, you can upgrade it in place rather than creating a new one. Upgrading removes the monthly caps and the credit limit while keeping your resources and subscription ID. There is no reason to upgrade during this course; save that step until you are deploying something you want to keep running at scale.

Summary

The Azure free account gives you a real subscription, a one-time credit, and monthly free allowances — everything you need to follow this course without spending money. You created the account, verified your subscription, and set a budget alert to protect yourself. In the next chapter you will get started with Microsoft Foundry inside that subscription.

Creating the Azure Free Account

Creating an Azure free account takes about ten minutes and is entirely self-service. This chapter walks you through the sign-up flow step by step, explains the verification steps so they do not surprise you, and covers the most common problems people hit along the way.

What You Need Before You Start

Have these ready before you open the sign-up page:

  • A Microsoft account. If you have an Outlook, Hotmail, or Xbox account, you can use it. Otherwise the sign-up page will help you create one from any email address.
  • A phone number that can receive a code or call.
  • A credit or debit card. It will be used to verify your identity; you will not be charged for free usage.
  • Your name and address matching the card.

Step 1: Start at the Sign-Up Page

Open your browser and go to the Azure sign-up page (look for the "Create your Azure free account" page from the main Azure website, or azure.microsoft.com/free). You will land on an overview page describing the free benefits you met in the previous chapter.

  1. Click Start free (sometimes labeled Try Azure for free).
  2. If prompted, sign in with your Microsoft account. If you do not have one, follow the Create one link and complete the account setup first, then return.
  3. When asked whether this is a new or existing subscription, choose to start a new free account.

Step 2: Verify Your Identity

Azure asks for two pieces of verification. These are standard anti-fraud checks, not billing events:

  1. Phone verification. Enter your country code and phone number, choose text message or voice call, and enter the code you receive.
  2. Card verification. Enter the card details and your billing address. Azure may make a small temporary pre-authorization (a few dollars or the local equivalent) that is held briefly and released — you are not charged.

The card pre-authorization can appear on your bank statement as a pending charge for a short time. It is an authorization hold, not a purchase. If it has not disappeared within a few days, your bank's hold window determines when it drops off — contact them only if it is charged, rather than held.

Step 3: Agree to the Agreement and Finish

After verification you will see the Azure subscription agreement and any consent prompts for data sharing. Read the agreement, tick the consent boxes you are comfortable with, and click to create the subscription. The portal will show a confirmation that your subscription is ready. From here you can go straight into the Azure portal, which the next chapter tours.

Save the confirmation page if it offers one, or bookmark portal.azure.com and note the sign-in email. From now on, that email address plus your Microsoft account password is the key to everything you create.

Step 4: Confirm Your Benefits

Once the account exists, it is worth confirming the free benefits are attached. In the Azure portal, open the subscription page for your new subscription. You should see the free-account label and, in the cost-management area, information about your credit and free-service allowances. If the credit does not appear, give it a few minutes to propagate before troubleshooting.

Common Sign-Up Problems

Most sign-up friction comes from a small set of causes. Here is how to handle each:

  • "Verification failed" on the card: the card must support international transactions and be in an eligible country. Try a different card, or verify the billing address matches the card exactly.
  • Phone verification rejects your number: VoIP numbers often fail. Try a mobile number on a regular carrier.
  • Your country or region is not supported for free accounts: free-account eligibility is regional. Without it you would need a paid subscription instead.
  • You already have an Azure subscription: the free-account benefits are typically available once per person. You can still use your existing subscription, but the course assumes the free tier.

If you sign up using an organizational or work email, the subscription may be governed by your employer's policies, and you may not have full control over what you create. For this course, a personal Microsoft account keeps things simple and under your control.

Summary

You signed up for an Azure free account by signing in with a Microsoft account, verifying your phone and a card, and accepting the subscription agreement. The card was only used for identity verification, and your free benefits are now active. Next you will explore the Azure portal, the control center where all of your resources — including your future AI deployments — live.

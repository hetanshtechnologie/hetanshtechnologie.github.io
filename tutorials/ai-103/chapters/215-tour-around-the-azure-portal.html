Tour around the Azure portal

Now that you have an account, you need to find your way around. The Azure portal at portal.azure.com is the web console where you create, inspect, configure, and delete Azure resources. This chapter tours its main areas so that the labs later in the course do not feel like wandering through a strange building.

The Portal Home Page

When you sign in you land on the portal home page. It has a few key zones:

  • A global search bar at the top that finds services, resources, and documentation.
  • A sidebar on the left with quick navigation to services you use often.
  • A set of shortcut tiles in the center for common starting points such as Resource groups, All resources, and Virtual machines.
  • A notification bell and a settings gear in the top-right corner.

You can pin your favorite pages to the dashboard so the home page shows exactly what you care about. For this course, the two shortcuts you will use most are Resource groups and All resources.

The Global Search Bar

The search bar is the fastest way to move around the portal. Type a few letters of a service name — for example "AI Foundry" or "resource group" — and the portal offers matching services, marketplace items, resources, and even documentation links. Making search your default navigation habit beats hunting through menus.

When a lab says "open Azure AI Foundry" or "go to resource groups," the quickest route is almost always the global search bar at the top of the portal, not the sidebar.

Resource Groups

Open Resource groups from the home page or search bar. This page lists every resource group in your subscription. Clicking into one shows the resources it contains, with each resource's name, type, region, and status. This page is also where you create a new resource group and where you delete one — deleting a resource group deletes everything inside it, which makes it your primary cleanup tool throughout the course.

All Resources

The All resources page lists every resource you own across all resource groups, in one flat table. When you create many resources, this view helps you find a specific one without remembering which group it lives in. Selecting a resource from the list opens its resource page — a dedicated management page for that single resource, with overview, settings, monitoring, and identity sections.

The Marketplace

The Marketplace is Azure's catalog of services and solution templates you can deploy. It includes Microsoft services, open-source software, and partner products. In practice, the marketplace is often reached indirectly: when you click Create for a service, the portal shows you the marketplace entry for that service. For this course, you will create resources directly through the search bar and service pages rather than browsing the marketplace.

Cloud Shell

The portal embeds a terminal called Cloud Shell, reachable from the toolbar icon that looks like a command prompt (>_). Cloud Shell gives you a bash or PowerShell environment running in Azure, with the az command-line tool pre-installed and already signed in. Every lab in this course can be done through the portal's point-and-click interface, but Cloud Shell is there when you prefer commands — and several labs show the az equivalent.

# In Cloud Shell, list all resource groups in your subscription
az group list --output table

Cloud Shell stores your working files in a small storage account it creates for you. That storage account is a real Azure resource and appears in your subscription. It costs essentially nothing at free-account scale, but you will see it listed and should not be confused by it.

Portal Settings

The gear icon opens portal settings, where you can adjust language, time zone, theme, and how notifications behave. Two settings are worth knowing about:

  • Startup view: choose what you see first when you sign in — the default home page or a page you pinned.
  • Menu behavior: choose between a docked sidebar and an overlay menu on small screens.

Sign Out and Security

Your portal session is tied to your Microsoft account. If you share a computer, sign out when you finish, and consider turning on multi-factor authentication on the account — the portal holds real credentials, so it deserves real protection. You can also manage who else can access specific resources through the Access control (IAM) blade on any resource page, which you will encounter again when you look at AI service security.

Summary

The Azure portal is organized around global search, resource groups, the all-resources list, and per-resource pages, with Cloud Shell providing a built-in command line. With the home page, search bar, resource groups, and settings under your belt, you can navigate any lab in this course. Next you will meet the family of AI services that Azure offers.

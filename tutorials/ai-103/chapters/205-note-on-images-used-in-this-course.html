Note on images used in this course

This short chapter explains the screenshots and diagrams you will see throughout the course, why they are included, and how to make the best use of them. The images serve a practical purpose — showing you what the interfaces look like — but they also need a note about accuracy, since the products behind them change over time.

Why Images Are Used

The course is hands-on, and a large part of every hands-on task happens inside web portals: the OpenAI API platform, the Azure portal, Microsoft Foundry, Azure AI Foundry, and various studio tools. Words alone are ambiguous when describing a button that lives inside a menu inside a pane. Screenshots and annotated diagrams show you exactly where to look, what to click, and what to expect on screen.

  • Portal screenshots show the expected layout and location of controls.
  • Diagrams illustrate architecture and data flow, which are hard to describe precisely in prose.
  • Code examples show the exact scripts to run, and those are always typed out in full alongside any image.

Images Reflect the Time They Were Captured

Cloud and AI products change quickly. Vendors routinely redesign portals, rename buttons, add menu items, and change default settings. Every screenshot in this course was captured at a particular moment, so your screen may not match it exactly.

If a button you need is not where the screenshot shows it, do not assume the chapter is wrong. Look for the same function under a slightly different name or location — the steps and the underlying workflow remain valid even when the interface rearranges.

What to Do When Your Screen Looks Different

  1. Read the surrounding text first. The narrative usually describes the intent of the step, not just the click.
  2. Search the portal for the named control or feature using its built-in search box.
  3. Check the version number or "What's new" page of the product if one is available.
  4. If you cannot find a feature at all, it may have moved to a different plan, a different region, or a preview state. The course notes call this out wherever it is known.

Copyright and Redistribution

The screenshots show vendor interfaces and are included for educational commentary — the same way a textbook quotes and illustrates a product. You are welcome to study them and to capture your own equivalent screenshots for personal notes. What you may not do is republish this course's images as your own material or use them commercially without permission.

Do not crop or strip any branding or copyright markings from images before using them, and never present vendor-owned interface captures as your own creative work.

Zoom and Retina Displays

Portal screenshots are compressed to keep page sizes reasonable. On small or high-resolution screens, use your browser's zoom (Ctrl and +, or Cmd and + on a Mac) to enlarge the images and read labels clearly.

The best companion to any screenshot is your own machine. When a chapter walks through a portal, try to follow along live with a browser window beside the book. Your own screen is the most accurate guide of all.

Summary

Images in this course show portal layouts, architecture diagrams, and code output. They were captured at a specific time, so they may differ from what you see today — treat them as a guide, not a guarantee. Read the narrative, search for renamed controls, and keep your own window open to follow along. Next, the chapter on OpenAI's model lineup shows you what the different models are for.

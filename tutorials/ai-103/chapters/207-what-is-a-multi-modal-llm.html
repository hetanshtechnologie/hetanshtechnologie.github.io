What is a Multi-modal LLM

Most of what you have read so far describes language models that work with text. A multi-modal LLM goes further: it can accept and understand more than one type of input — text, images, audio, and sometimes video — within a single model. This chapter explains what multi-modal means, how it works, and what it unlocks.

What "Modality" Means

A modality is a type of data or sense — text, image, audio, video. A text-only model understands one modality. A multi-modal model can consume several of them together. The most common combination in practice is text plus images: you send a picture along with your question, and the model answers about what it can see.

How a Multi-modal Model Sees Images

A model cannot look at pixels the way you do. Instead, the model divides an image into small patches and converts each patch into a numeric representation, then feeds those representations into the same attention-based architecture that processes text. The text of your question and the image's numeric representation are processed together, which lets the model relate what you asked to what it sees in the picture.

When you send an image through an API, the image travels as either a public URL or a base64-encoded data string inside the message. The model handles the decoding and analysis internally — from your code's point of view, the image is just another piece of content attached to the request.

What Multi-modal Models Can Do

  • Describe images: "What is happening in this photo?"
  • Read documents: extract text, tables, and structured details from PDFs and scanned pages.
  • Explain screenshots: "What error is this screen showing, and how do I fix it?"
  • Answer about diagrams and charts: "What does this chart say about sales in Q3?"
  • Transcribe and reason about audio when the model supports audio input.

In this course you used exactly this capability earlier — the lab that sent images as context to a chat model, and the content understanding chapters that analysed documents and invoices, all rely on multi-modal understanding.

Text and Vision Working Together

The real power is the combination. A model that can both read and see can be pointed at real-world material and asked natural questions about it. For example, you can send a photograph of a restaurant receipt and ask: "What did we spend on drinks, and what was the total with tax?" The model reads the text in the image, reasons over it, and returns the answer — a task impossible for a text-only model and far beyond a simple optical character reader.

Practical Considerations

  • Image size and resolution: larger images consume more tokens, raising cost and latency. Resize or crop when the full detail is not needed.
  • Cost: image input is priced per image, and high-resolution images cost more than low-resolution ones.
  • Limits: accuracy on images is good but not perfect — blurry, dark, or rotated images can defeat the model.
  • Not all models are multi-modal: check the model's capabilities before assuming it can accept images; a text-only model will reject image input.

For document-heavy work, keep the question specific. "Extract the invoice number, total, and due date as JSON" will give you a better result than "tell me about this document." Specify exactly what you want the model to find.

Summary

A multi-modal LLM processes several input types — text, images, audio, and sometimes video — in a single model. It sees images by converting them into numeric patch representations processed alongside text, which enables describing, reading, and reasoning over visual content.

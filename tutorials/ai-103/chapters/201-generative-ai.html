Generative AI

The previous chapter described machine learning systems that classify and predict. Generative AI is a different, newer branch of the field: instead of deciding what something is, a generative model decides what something could be — and then creates it. Where a classifier looks at a photo and says "this is a dog," a generative model looks at the words "a happy dog running through a park" and paints the picture. This chapter explains how generative AI works, what kinds of content it can produce, and why it behaves the way it does.

What Generative AI Is

Generative AI is the branch of artificial intelligence concerned with producing new content. The content can be text, images, audio, video, or code, and it is produced in response to a request — usually a prompt written in plain language. The defining idea is that the model has learned the statistical patterns of a large body of training material, and it can now sample from those patterns to synthesise something that resembles the material but is not a copy of it.

Generative models are not simply retrieving answers from a database. When you ask for a story about a lost astronaut, the model assembles a new arrangement of words that fits the pattern of stories it has seen. The result is original in the sense that this exact combination of words was almost certainly never written before.

Generative Versus Discriminative Models

Classical machine learning models are often called discriminative: they learn the boundary between categories and then classify new inputs.

AspectDiscriminative modelGenerative model
Core questionWhat is this?Create something new
Typical outputA label, score, or categoryText, image, audio, video, code
Example taskIs this email spam?Write a marketing email
ExamplesImage classifiers, fraud detectorsLLMs, image generators

The two are not mutually exclusive — a generative model can also be used discriminatively, for example by asking a language model to classify a sentence. But their natural strength is different, and understanding which family you are dealing with explains a lot about its behaviour.

How Generative Models Are Built

There are several architectural approaches to generative AI, and they have evolved over time:

ApproachHow it generatesMain use
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)Two networks — a generator and a critic — compete; the generator improves by trying to fool the criticEarly image generation, style transfer
Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)Learn a compressed representation of data and sample new examples from itImage and data generation
Diffusion modelsLearn to reverse the process of adding noise, gradually turning random noise into a clear imageModern image generation
Transformer-based language modelsPredict the next token in a sequence, guided by an attention mechanismText generation, chat assistants, code

Modern assistants like ChatGPT combine the last of these approaches with a conversation-friendly interface. The underlying model is a transformer that predicts text, and the product wraps it with instructions about how to behave in a chat.

What Generative AI Can Create

  • Text: essays, summaries, translations, emails, product descriptions, and answers to questions.
  • Code: functions, tests, explanations of existing code, and conversion between programming languages.
  • Images: pictures from a text description, edits to existing images, and style transfers.
  • Audio: synthetic voices, music, and sound effects.
  • Video: short video clips generated from text prompts.

Creativity Versus Reliability

Because generative models sample from probability distributions, they are inherently a little bit random. The same prompt can produce different outputs on different runs, and the amount of variety can be tuned with settings such as temperature. A low temperature makes output more predictable; a high temperature makes it more creative.

That same probabilistic nature is the source of the models' most famous weakness: they can produce confident-sounding statements that are wrong. When a model states something that looks plausible but is not true, it is called a hallucination. The model is not lying — it is generating text that follows the statistical patterns of its training data — but the effect is the same from the reader's perspective.

Never assume a generative model's factual claims are correct. For anything that matters — dates, numbers, legal text, medical advice — verify against a trusted source, or better yet, ground the model in your own data using retrieval augmented generation.

From Generative AI to ChatGPT

ChatGPT is the product that made generative AI mainstream. It is a chat interface wrapped around a large language model, and it is the natural next topic in this course. Before that, though, you will learn about large language models themselves — the engine that makes all of this possible.

Summary

Generative AI is the branch of machine learning that creates new content by sampling from the patterns it learned during training. It contrasts with discriminative models, which classify existing data, and it is built with approaches ranging from GANs and VAEs to diffusion models and transformer-based language models. Its strengths — fluent text, images, code, and audio — come with a real limitation: output is probabilistic and can be wrong.

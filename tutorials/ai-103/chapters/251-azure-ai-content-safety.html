Azure AI Content Safety

Generative applications can produce content that is inappropriate, harmful, or misleading — and they can be manipulated into doing so. Building responsibly means putting guardrails around both what goes into a model and what comes out of it. Azure AI Content Safety is the Azure service for that job. This chapter explains what it detects, how it scores content, and where it fits in an application.

Why Content Safety Exists

A foundation model is trained on the open internet, and its output reflects that breadth. Left unguarded, a chat application can return material that harms users or violates policy, and an attacker can steer it to do so on purpose. Content safety provides a defense that is:

  • Consistent: the same thresholds apply to every request, independent of which model produced the text.
  • Layered: it complements the model's own safety tuning instead of replacing it.
  • Verifiable: every decision comes back with a category and a severity score you can log and audit.

What It Detects

Azure AI Content Safety analyzes text and images against a set of harm categories. The primary categories are:

CategoryWhat it covers
Hate and fairnessSlurs, discriminatory language, and attacks on groups
SexualSexually explicit or suggestive content
ViolenceDescriptions or depictions of violence and harm
Self-harmContent encouraging or describing self-injury

Each analysis returns, per category, a severity score and a level. The severity scale runs from 0 (no content detected) through mild, moderate, and severe — and you decide which threshold is acceptable for your application.

Severity thresholds are a product decision, not just a technical one. A children's app might reject anything above mild; a news analysis tool might tolerate moderate mentions of violence when discussing current events. The service gives you the score; your policy decides what to do with it.

Prompt Shields: Defending the Input Side

Beyond moderating content, the service protects against a distinct class of attack: prompt injection. Two attacks are common:

  • Direct injection: a user writes "ignore your previous instructions and..." inside their own message.
  • Indirect injection: malicious instructions are hidden inside a document, web page, or email that your application processes and passes to the model.

Prompt Shields is the Content Safety feature designed to detect these. It flags user inputs that try to override system instructions (the jailbreak detection) and content that contains hidden instructions embedded in untrusted documents (the document detection). For RAG applications in particular, scanning retrieved documents with Prompt Shields closes a real gap: the model is happy to follow instructions it found in its context window.

How It Integrates

Content safety can be used in several ways, from zero-code to fully programmatic:

  • Deployment-level filters in Foundry: default filters come with many model deployments, and you can create custom filter configurations that block output at defined severity thresholds.
  • The Content Safety studio: a portal for testing content against your chosen thresholds without writing code.
  • The SDKs: direct calls to the moderation endpoints from Python, C#, and other languages, giving you per-category scores to act on in your own logic.

In a typical pipeline, input is scanned before it reaches the model and output is scanned after it returns — a "both sides" defense that most production systems adopt.

Moderate on both ends. Scanning the prompt stops an attacker from steering the model; scanning the response stops the model from producing something harmful even when the input was innocent. Each pass answers a different question.

Building a Simple Text Moderation Call

The pattern is deliberately simple — a single call with your text, a list of categories, and a threshold:

import os
from azure.ai.contentsafety import ContentSafetyClient
from azure.ai.contentsafety.models import AnalyzeTextOptions, TextCategory
from azure.core.credentials import AzureKeyCredential

client = ContentSafetyClient(
    endpoint=os.environ["CONTENT_SAFETY_ENDPOINT"],
    credential=AzureKeyCredential(os.environ["CONTENT_SAFETY_KEY"]),
)

response = client.analyze_text(
    AnalyzeTextOptions(
        text="An ordinary, safe sentence about today's weather.",
        categories=[TextCategory.HATE, TextCategory.VIOLENCE, TextCategory.SELF_HARM, TextCategory.SEXUAL],
    )
)

for result in response.categories_analysis:
    print(result.category, "->", result.severity)

Each category prints its severity, and you can block or flag based on your thresholds.

Content safety reduces risk; it does not eliminate it. New jailbreak techniques appear constantly, and no filter is perfect. Treat moderation as one layer in a responsible-AI posture that also includes human review for sensitive scenarios, logging, and an incident-response plan.

Summary

Azure AI Content Safety scans text and images for hate, sexual, violence, and self-harm content, reporting severity scores you can act on with your own policy thresholds. Prompt Shields adds protection against direct and indirect prompt injection, which matters especially for RAG applications that feed untrusted documents to the model.

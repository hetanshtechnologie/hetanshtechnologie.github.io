Text to Speech

Text to Speech completes the voice arc in the opposite direction: it takes written text and produces spoken audio. Where the last two chapters covered listening, this chapter covers speaking. You will learn what makes neural voices sound natural, how to control the voice with markup, and how to choose between the output formats and voices available in Azure AI Speech.

From Text to Audio: How TTS Works

A modern neural TTS system does not glue together pre-recorded syllables. It trains a deep neural network to predict audio from text in two stages. The first stage, often called the frontend, converts the raw text into a phonetic representation — deciding how to pronounce words, where to pause, and which syllables to stress. The second stage, the acoustic model, turns that representation into a continuous waveform.

Because the network is generating audio rather than playing recordings, it can speak text it has never seen, change its pace, and convey emotion. The trade-off is subtle variability: the same sentence rendered twice will not produce bit-identical audio. If your application needs identical audio on every run, you render once and cache the result.

Voices: Prebuilt, Custom, and Multilingual

Azure offers a large catalog of prebuilt neural voices. Each voice has a name, a language, and a personality — for example a male or female voice of a certain age with a particular regional accent. You select a voice simply by naming it in the request.

When the prebuilt set is not enough, you can train a custom neural voice. You record and upload a dataset of clean studio speech from a target speaker, train the model on that data, and deploy a voice that sounds like that person. Custom voices are used for brand identities, character narration, and accessibility experiences where a specific known voice matters.

SSML: Controlling More Than the Words

Plain text tells the service what to say; SSML tells it how to say it. Speech Synthesis Markup Language is an XML-based format that wraps your text with tags controlling prosody and delivery:

  • <prosody> for speaking rate, pitch, and volume.
  • <break> for deliberate pauses of a specified length.
  • <emphasis> to stress particular words.
  • <phoneme> to force a pronunciation for unusual names or words.
  • <mstts:express-as> for expressive styles such as cheerful, empathetic, or newscast.

SSML turns a robotic announcement into something that can read a sales pitch with energy, or a diagnosis with care. The trade-off is that you are now authoring markup, so the text-to-speech step becomes a small template-rendering job in your application.

Start with plain text and only reach for SSML when a specific effect matters. A handful of tags — <break>, <prosody>, and an expressive style — cover the vast majority of realistic needs, and plain text is far easier to maintain.

Output Formats and Streaming

The service can return audio in several encodings, including RIFF WAV and compressed formats such as MP3 and OGG Opus. Lower sample rates produce smaller files at some loss of clarity, so pick the lightest format that still sounds right for your target device. You can also stream the audio as it is generated rather than waiting for the whole clip, which lets playback begin almost immediately.

For applications that build audio from many pieces — a news reader composing an hourly bulletin, or a game that narrates dynamic events — streaming each segment as it is produced keeps perceived latency low even when the total audio is long.

When to Render, When to Cache

TTS has a cost per character of audio generated, and it is also one of the slower operations in a speech pipeline because it must synthesize a waveform. Two habits keep costs and latency down:

  • Cache audio for any text that is repeated, such as welcome messages or product descriptions.
  • Render only the parts of a page that change, not the whole page every time.

Summary

Text to Speech synthesizes natural audio from text using neural voice models. You choose a prebuilt or custom voice, optionally shape delivery with SSML, and receive audio in the encoding your device needs.

Anatomy of a SOAR Platform

Although SOAR vendors differ in UX and pricing, they share a common architecture. Understanding these layers lets you reason about any platform and design playbooks effectively:

Key Concept — Artifacts: In SOAR platforms, an artifact is a discrete piece of evidence extracted from an alert — an IP, domain, file hash, email address, or username. Playbooks act on artifacts. Normalizing artifacts (e.g., always extracting file.sha256, destination.ip) is what makes enrichment and automation reusable.

Connectors and Actions

A connector is the integration between SOAR and an external tool. It defines the actions the SOAR can invoke. For an EDR, actions might be: Get File Reputation, Isolate Host, Search Process. For a firewall: Block IP, Unblock IP.

  • On-prem connectors: run inside your network (or on-prem SOAR) to reach internal tools.
  • Cloud/SaaS connectors: use authenticated APIs (OAuth/API keys) to reach cloud services.
  • Custom connectors: you write wrappers (usually Python) around tools with no prebuilt integration — the "automation anywhere" layer.
Security Note: SOAR connectors carry powerful credentials (firewall admin, AD admin). Treat the SOAR as a critical asset: vault all secrets, use least-privilege service accounts, restrict who can edit playbooks, and audit all actions. A compromised SOAR is a crown-jewel target.

Case Management: The Response Layer

Case management turns alerts and automation into a manageable incident. A well-designed case includes:

  • Identity: unique ID, title, source system, severity, priority.
  • Context: affected assets, users, IOCs, enriched intelligence.
  • Workflow: status lifecycle (New → Triage → Investigating → Contained → Resolved → Closed), task lists, assignments.
  • Evidence: attached screenshots, logs, tool output, timestamps.
  • Timeline: chronological record of all events and actions.
  • Integration: two-way sync with ITSM (Jira, ServiceNow), email notifications, team chat.

Case management is also where analyst workloads get balanced and where SOC managers measure performance. A SOAR without good case management is just a workflow tool.

Deployment Models

  • SaaS: Vendor-hosted, zero maintenance, fastest time-to-value. Data leaves your network (evaluate for sensitive logs).
  • On-prem / self-hosted: Full control, common in regulated industries. You own upgrades and scaling.
  • Integrated: Built into your SIEM/XDR (e.g., Microsoft Sentinel Automation, Splunk SOAR alongside Splunk). Less to manage, but ties you to the platform.
  • Open source: Shuffle, TheHive+Cortex, dfir-iris. Powerful for learning and cost-sensitive teams; you build much of it yourself.

Choosing a SOAR Platform

Evaluate along these dimensions:

  1. Integration coverage: does it connect to the SIEM, EDR, firewall, email, ITSM, and intel tools you already run?
  2. Playbook developer experience: no-code vs code; does your team have the skills?
  3. Case management quality: is the response workflow usable by analysts daily?
  4. Automation power: loops, custom code, error handling, human approvals, concurrency.
  5. Security & compliance: RBAC, audit, encryption, data residency, certifications.
  6. Total cost: license + consumption + connectors + maintenance + staff.
  7. Vendor health and community: marketplace, docs, support, community playbooks.

Integrated vs Standalone SOAR

The market is shifting toward integrated automation inside SIEM/XDR platforms. Integrated SOAR wins on simplicity and cost for single-vendor shops; standalone SOAR (XSOAR, Splunk SOAR) wins where orchestration breadth, custom automation, or multi-platform independence matter. Many large enterprises run a primary SIEM and a standalone SOAR on top. Your choice should follow your detection stack, not the other way around.

Practice Task: Create a comparison matrix for three SOAR options (one SaaS, one integrated, one open source) against your org's top 10 integrations and top 5 playbook requirements. Score each, pick a winner, and write a one-page deployment plan that includes: which 3 playbooks to build first, who owns the platform, and how you will measure MTTR improvement after 90 days.

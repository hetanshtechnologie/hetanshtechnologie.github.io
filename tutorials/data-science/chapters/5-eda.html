5. Exploratory Data Analysis

Chapter 5

What is EDA?

Exploratory data analysis (EDA) is the disciplined process of getting to know your data before modeling: summarizing it, finding patterns, spotting problems, and generating hypotheses. EDA answers "what is going on here?" so that modeling answers "how do I use it?".

Summary Statistics

import pandas as pd
df = pd.read_parquet("data/clean/customers.parquet")

# Numeric summary
print(df.describe())

# Central tendency
print(df["spend"].mean(), df["spend"].median(), df["spend"].mode())

# Spread
print(df["spend"].std(), df["spend"].var())
print(df["spend"].quantile([0.25, 0.5, 0.75]))

# Counts for categoricals
print(df["city"].value_counts(normalize=True))
Mean vs median: the median resists outliers. When income and spend are skewed (a few very large values), the median often describes the "typical" customer better than the mean. Always report both.

Skewness & Distributions

skew = df["spend"].skew()
print(skew)   # > 1: heavily right-skewed; < -1: left-skewed

# Compare distribution to a normal (histogram + KDE)
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns
sns.histplot(df["spend"], kde=True)
plt.title("Distribution of Spend")
plt.show()

Right-skewed data is extremely common in business (spending, wait times, incomes). Common fixes include log transforms (np.log1p(df["spend"])) before modeling.

Grouped Analysis (Split-Apply-Combine)

Compare segments — this is where most actionable insight lives.

# Average spend by city
print(df.groupby("city")["spend"].agg(["mean", "median", "count"]))

# Spend by city and plan
print(df.groupby(["city", "plan"])["spend"].mean().unstack())

# Cross-tabulation of two categoricals
print(pd.crosstab(df["plan"], df["is_churned"], normalize="index"))

Correlations

# Pairwise Pearson correlation
corr = df[["spend", "age", "tenure", "support_tickets"]].corr()
print(corr)

# Rank correlation (robust to outliers / monotonic but non-linear)
print(df[["spend", "age"]].corr(method="spearman"))
Correlation is not causation. A correlation between two columns may be confounded by a third variable, may be pure chance, or may run in a direction you cannot see from the data alone. EDA finds candidates; experiments (Chapter 8) establish cause.

Comparing Distributions Across Groups

# Box plots: distribution by category
sns.boxplot(x="plan", y="spend", data=df)

# Violin plots show the full shape
sns.violinplot(x="plan", y="spend", data=df)

Hypothesis Generation in EDA

Every pattern you find should be turned into a testable claim:

  • Finding: "Enterprise-plan customers spend 2x more than Basic."
  • Question: Is the difference real or due to sample size/outliers?
  • Design: A t-test (Chapter 8) between the two groups.

EDA Workflow

  1. Profile: shape, types, missingness, duplicates.
  2. Univariate: distributions of each column.
  3. Bivariate: relationships and segment comparisons.
  4. Multivariate: correlations, cross-tabs, interactions.
  5. Document: capture findings and questions in your notebook.

9. Stakeholder Management & Consulting Mindset

Getting Buy-In

The Stakeholder Map

A Cost TakeOut engagement touches many stakeholders with different agendas. Alignment is not optional; it is the difference between recommendations that are adopted and recommendations that sit in a drawer.

StakeholderWhat They Care AboutHow to Engage
ProcurementProcess, supplier relationships, workloadCo-design approach; protect their relationships
FinanceVerified savings, baseline integrity, cashAgree savings definitions and tracking early
LegalTerms, liability, complianceBring contract issues early, not at signing
OperationsContinuity, quality, serviceInvolve in specs, cutover, and contingency
Business / BU leadersBudget, performance, riskLink savings to their P&L and objectives

Influencing Senior Executives

Senior leaders make decisions on clarity and confidence. To influence them:

  • Lead with the recommendation — state the "so what" first, then the evidence.
  • Quantify everything — savings, cost, risk, and timeline in numbers.
  • Show options — present 2–3 paths with trade-offs, and a clear recommendation.
  • Pre-empt objections — address the top three concerns before they are raised.
  • Commit to accountability — define owners, milestones, and tracking.

Problem Structuring

Consultants are paid to structure ambiguity. The standard toolkit:

  • Issue trees — break a big question ("how do we take out 10% cost?") into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive (MECE) branches.
  • Hypothesis-driven work — state a testable hypothesis and design analyses to prove or disprove it, rather than boiling the ocean.
  • 80/20 thinking — focus effort where the value is; perfect the few analyses that matter.
  • Driver-based models — tie cost to drivers so you can model sensitivity and scenarios.
  • Storylining — structure output as an argument: context, insight, recommendation, action.
Consulting Mindset: Be independent in judgment (say what the data shows, even if unpopular), collaborative in delivery, and always leaving the client better able to manage without you. "You are the expert, not the order-taker."

Communication That Lands

Tailor communication to the audience:

  • Executive committee — 3-page story with recommendation, value, risk, ask
  • Functional owners — detailed workplans, process maps, responsibility matrices
  • Working teams — clear tasks, owners, deadlines, checkpoints
  • Written — pyramid structure: conclusion first, supporting points below
Real-World Example: A sourcing recommendation to switch logistics providers is blocked by the Operations director who fears disruption. Instead of pushing, the team builds a pilot with the new provider on two low-risk routes, presents operations with a continuity plan, and secures a phased rollout. Six months later the full transition is adopted — with the director as a sponsor.
Practice Task: Draw an issue tree for "reduce total indirect cost by 12% in 12 months." Then write the executive summary (200 words) with a recommendation, quantified value, key risks, and an ask.

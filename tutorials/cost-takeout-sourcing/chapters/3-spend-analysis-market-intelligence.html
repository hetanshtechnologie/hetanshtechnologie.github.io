3. Spend Analysis & Supply Market Intelligence

The Evidence Base

Why Spend Analysis Comes First

Every credible Cost TakeOut engagement starts with spend analysis. It establishes the baseline, prioritizes categories, quantifies opportunity, and provides the evidence that executives trust. A spend analysis that cannot stand up to a CFO's questions is worse than no analysis at all.

Golden Rule: No savings claim, no sourcing strategy, and no negotiation target is credible without a clean, defensible spend baseline.

The Spend Analysis Process

  1. Collect data — pull spend from ERP (e.g., SAP, Oracle), P2P systems, AP data, and manual records.
  2. Clean and normalize — fix supplier name variants ("IBM", "IBM Corp", "International Business Machines"), map to master supplier and category codes, and reconcile currencies.
  3. Classify — assign every transaction to a category and sub-category using UNSPSC or a custom taxonomy.
  4. Analyze — build the spend cube: by category, supplier, business unit, plant, cost center, and time period.
  5. Quantify opportunity — estimate potential savings per category using benchmarks, should-cost, and market data.
  6. Visualize — produce dashboards that executives can interrogate, not static spreadsheets.

Key Analyses

  • Spend concentration — Pareto analysis: which suppliers and categories drive 80% of spend.
  • Supplier count — how many suppliers per category and whether consolidation is possible.
  • Price variance — different units paying different prices for the same item or service.
  • Contract coverage — what percentage of spend is under contract versus off-contract (maverick spend).
  • Demand analysis — volume trends, seasonality, and whether demand itself can be reduced or shifted.
  • Compliance — spend that bypasses preferred suppliers, catalogs, or negotiated rates.

Supply Market Intelligence

Market intelligence (MI) informs sourcing strategy by answering: Who are the potential suppliers? What is the industry structure and pricing dynamic? Where are the capacity and risk hot spots? Sources of MI include supplier RFIs, industry reports, trade associations, commodity price indices, customs data, and expert interviews.

MI DimensionQuestions to Answer
Market structureFragmented or consolidated? Barriers to entry? Substitute products?
Cost driversRaw materials, labor, energy, logistics — what moves the price?
Pricing benchmarksIndexation, spot vs contract pricing, landed cost comparisons
Supply riskGeopolitical, financial, quality, capacity, single-source dependencies
InnovationNew suppliers, new technologies, alternative materials

From Savings, Risk, and Value

Spend analysis should surface three types of opportunity simultaneously:

  • Savings — price reduction, demand management, specification change, competition, consolidation.
  • Risk — single-source dependencies, financially weak suppliers, non-compliant suppliers, supply continuity gaps.
  • Value — better payment terms, innovation, sustainability (ESG), reduced total cost of ownership, working capital improvement.
Real-World Example: During a spend analysis for a logistics-heavy client, the team discovers 47 separate courier accounts, three different negotiated rate cards, and ₹4 crore of un-invoiced volume under no contract. Consolidation onto two providers with a single rate card and an automated shipment-to-invoice reconciliation delivers 14% savings plus working capital gains from improved terms.
Practice Task: Build a simple spend cube in a spreadsheet for a sample dataset (category, supplier, amount, month). Run a Pareto analysis, identify price variance across cost centers, and calculate the share of spend under contract. Present three prioritized savings hypotheses.

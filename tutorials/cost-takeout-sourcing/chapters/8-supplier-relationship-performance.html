8. Supplier Relationship & Performance Management

Protecting and Growing Value

Why Supplier Management Matters

Negotiated savings can be eroded within months if suppliers underperform or relationships sour. Supplier Relationship Management (SRM) and supplier performance management protect the value you captured, manage risk, and unlock future value through collaboration and innovation.

Key Idea: Save money at negotiation, but protect it in the relationship. Cost TakeOut that ignores suppliers is like building savings on sand.

Segmented SRM

Invest relationship effort proportional to importance:

TierSuppliersRelationship Focus
StrategicCritical, high-spend, high-riskExecutive sponsors, joint business plans, innovation, value engineering
KeySignificant spend or dependencyQuarterly reviews, performance dashboards, improvement plans
CommodityStandard goods/servicesSelf-service, transactional, annual review

Performance Management

Performance is managed through well-designed SLAs and KPIs. Effective KPIs are few, measurable, and tied to outcomes:

  • Quality — defect rate, first-pass yield, rejection rate
  • Delivery — on-time in-full (OTIF), lead time, fill rate
  • Cost — price adherence, cost reduction year-on-year
  • Service — response time, resolution time, uptime
  • Compliance — regulatory, ESG, audit pass rate
  • Innovation — ideas implemented, co-developed solutions

Scorecards should be discussed in joint reviews, with evidence, not email chains. Remediation follows a clear ladder: discuss at review, issue formal improvement plan, escalate, and only then re-source.

Supply Risk & Risk Mitigation

Risk management is a core responsibility of the supplier-facing advisor:

  • Financial risk — monitor supplier financial health (credit reports, payment behavior)
  • Concentration risk — single-source dependencies; build dual sourcing
  • Operational risk — capacity, quality incidents, industrial action
  • Geopolitical & environmental — sanctions, tariffs, natural disasters, climate
  • Compliance risk — labor practices, corruption, data protection, ESG
Mitigation toolbox: risk segmentation, contingency and dual sourcing plans, safety stock and buffer capacity, contractual remedies and exit plans, supplier audits, and early-warning monitoring dashboards.

Supplier Development

Supplier development helps key suppliers improve so both parties win: sharing forecast visibility, co-investing in tooling or digitalization, providing training or process audits, and agreeing annual value-engineering targets. In return, buyers secure preferential pricing, capacity, and innovation access.

Real-World Example: A strategic packaging supplier delivers 92% OTIF. The client introduces a joint scorecard with OTIF, defect, and cost-down targets, agrees forecast visibility and a value-engineering roadmap, and runs quarterly business reviews with executive sponsors. OTIF reaches 98% and the supplier proposes 3 cost-down initiatives worth 4% — value that never appears in a price negotiation.
Practice Task: Design a supplier scorecard for a strategic supplier: choose six KPIs, define the data source for each, set targets, and outline the review cadence and remediation ladder.

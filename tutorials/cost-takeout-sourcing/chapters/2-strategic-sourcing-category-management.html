2. Strategic Sourcing & Category Management

The Foundation

What is Strategic Sourcing

Strategic sourcing is a systematic and data-driven approach to planning and managing an organization's supply base so that spend is optimized over time — considering price, quality, service, innovation, and risk — rather than simply buying from the lowest bidder. It is the opposite of tactical purchasing, which reacts to requisitions, and the foundation of any sustainable Cost TakeOut program.

Key Idea: Tactical buying optimizes a single transaction. Strategic sourcing optimizes the entire category, supply base, and total cost of ownership over the contract lifecycle.

Category Management

Category management organizes procurement activity around logical groups of similar spend — for example, packaging, IT hardware, logistics, marketing, or professional services — and assigns a dedicated strategy to each group. It shifts the team from "what do we buy today" to "how do we buy this category best over the next three years."

Direct vs Indirect Spend

TypeDefinitionExamplesFocus
Direct SpendCosts tied to the product or service soldRaw materials, components, packaging, inbound freightCost of goods sold, quality, supply continuity
Indirect SpendCosts that support operations but are not in the productIT, facilities, travel, marketing, professional services, MROOpEx reduction, compliance, user adoption

Most mature organizations address direct spend first because the savings pool is large, but indirect spend frequently holds 15–25% savings opportunity because it has historically been under-managed.

Supplier Segmentation

Not all suppliers deserve the same treatment. The classic Kraljic matrix segments purchases by supply risk / complexity and profit / spend impact:

QuadrantCharacteristicsSourcing Approach
RoutineLow risk, low value (e.g., stationery)Simplify: catalogs, automation, consolidated buying
LeverageLow risk, high value (e.g., commodities)Aggressively negotiate; run competitive RFx
BottleneckHigh risk, low value (e.g., specialized spare parts)Secure supply: dual sourcing, safety stock, long-term agreements
StrategicHigh risk, high value (e.g., key components)Partner: joint value creation, deep SRM, innovation

The 7-Step Strategic Sourcing Process

  1. Profile the category — define scope, spend baseline, stakeholders, and current state.
  2. Analyze the supply market — map suppliers, capacity, pricing dynamics, and risk factors.
  3. Develop the sourcing strategy — decide number of suppliers, contract types, and approach.
  4. Run the sourcing event — RFx, supplier qualification, evaluation, and negotiation.
  5. Select and negotiate — shortlist, negotiate commercial and legal terms, select winners.
  6. Transition and onboard — contract, supplier onboarding, implementation of pricing/process changes.
  7. Manage and improve — track savings, KPIs, and supplier performance; revisit annually.
Real-World Example: For a packaging category, the team profiles 40+ SKUs across 12 plants, discovers that 3 SKUs represent 60% of spend, segments the supply market into flexible suppliers, and runs a two-round e-auction. The outcome: two consolidated suppliers, standardized packaging designs, and 11% savings retained through annual indexation.
Practice Task: Take a category you buy at work or at home (e.g., office supplies or cloud services). Classify it on the Kraljic matrix and draft a one-page sourcing strategy with objectives, supplier options, and risks.
